**Re-derivation for C6.** Suppose A(f) + B(f/ρ) = A'(f) + B'(f/ρ) for every ρ in V. Let k = B' − B. Then A − A' = k(f/ρ) for every ρ in V, so k(ν) = k(ν·ρ_i/ρ_j). This means k is invariant under the multiplicative group generated by the speed ratios, and three cases follow:
- That group is trivial when |V| = 1: k is arbitrary, so no identifiability.
- It is cyclic when |V| = 2: k can be any log-periodic function.
- It is dense when there are two incommensurate log-ratios, or a continuum of speeds: a continuous k must be constant.

The derivation is correct. It is also the standard indeterminacy of spectral disentangling, so it is background, not a contribution.

**A consequence I had not written down (00:20).** It follows from C6 and matters for tonight's design. Fit to *one* traversal, with a free road term per segment, SCR does **not** identify the vehicle channel at all: any d(f) can be moved into the per-segment road terms B_seg(ν) − d(νρ_seg). The channel becomes identifiable only through:
- (a) the same road segment seen at more than one speed or by more than one vehicle (data), or
- (b) priors, such as the road being smooth along θ or a power-law road spectrum.

So single-trip "road" claims, whether from RoadScribe or from SCR, rest on priors, and cross-vehicle claims rest on data. Tonight's experiments must keep these two regimes apart.

**Further facts from P1**
- Road classes differ between two vehicles at the same place in 76% of pairs (by ≥ 2 classes in 14%). All three vehicles agree at only 4.5% of places.
- The weighted κ_road exceeds κ_felt even though exact agreement is lower. Road classes spread over more bands, so κ rewards rank consistency. Both views are reported.
- Variance shares (unique, type II) for log a_dec: location 23.8%, vehicle 18.2%, speed 0.3%, residual 2.1%. For log a_v: vehicle 18.8%. **RoadScribe's "road" index is no more vehicle-invariant than its "felt" index.**
- The residual (location × vehicle interaction plus noise) is only about 2%. The disagreement is therefore mostly a **systematic offset per vehicle**, which a gain calibration estimated on shared roads should remove. This is why P2-H3 matters.
- The within-location speed slope of log a_dec is 0.51. RoadScribe's γ = 0.35 removes well under half of the speed effect at a fixed place.
- Robustness: the same pattern holds without bump stretches (288 locations; γ̂_pooled −0.69, γ̂_within 0.84) and with 50 m stretches (632 locations; −0.65 / 0.81). It also holds per route: κ_road 0.66–0.74, vehicle share 15–20%.

**Interpretation, kept modest**
- (1) On roads the identifiability problem is real and large: one vehicle's "road" claim carries a vehicle signature worth about 18% of the variance.
- (2) The speed elasticity has the **wrong sign** when estimated across places within trips, a Simpson's-paradox pattern driven by endogenous speed. γ needs within-place variation, i.e. repeated traversals, to be identified. This is the cleanest concrete instance so far of "not identifiable from one traversal".
- (3) Most of the vehicle effect looks like a gain. If so, the fix is calibration on shared roads, not necessarily the full spectral factorisation (P2 will tell).
- Caveat: vehicle and driver are confounded, and there is one traversal per vehicle and route.

**Prior-art check after P1 (00:35)**
- *Road and vehicle separation from crowdsourced phones is established.* Botshekan et al., Data-Centric Engineering 2020 (the MIT "Carbin" line), estimate the road roughness PSD from smartphone vertical acceleration by an inverse random-vibration analysis with vehicle dynamics. They report that phone position matters only marginally.
- *Speed and vehicle dependence of vibration-based monitoring is well documented:* Stribling (TRB), multiple vehicle types and speeds; a 2023 study with speed corrections from taxi data; a 2024 deep-learning study validated on 29 vehicles.
- **So the SCR road estimator is not new even within the road domain.**
- *Not found in one search:* the specific confound that **drivers slow on rough stretches, so speed elasticity estimated across places has the wrong sign** (P1-H3b). That makes it the most promising "new fact" from tonight, but one search is not a literature review. It needs a proper check (driver speed choice versus pavement condition; endogeneity in crowdsourced roughness) before anything claims it.

**What P1–P3 say together (D16)**
1. **The problem is real and large on roads.** A single vehicle's "road" claim carries an 18% vehicle share; all three vehicles agree on the road class at only 4.5% of places; the speed effect has the wrong sign across places.
2. **The remedies that work are simple:**
   - a per-vehicle gain learned on *shared* roads (removes the vehicle main effect almost exactly; best held-out-vehicle accuracy, 97.3%);

4. **Consequence for the paper:** the contribution is the *identifiability measurement and its consequences for claims*, with simple, principled remedies. It is **not** a new estimator or architecture. That matches the 2026-11-11 verdict, now backed by data.

**Likely cause of SCR's bias.** Labelled as exploratory reasoning, not tested. The χ² log-bias correction ψ(3) − log 3 covers periodogram noise only. Real spectra also vary between frames within a 100 m stretch (events, surface changes), which adds a further Jensen gap. If that variability is lognormal with σ² of about 0.38, it would explain −0.095. But even with the bias removed, RMSE would be about √(0.196² − 0.095²) ≈ 0.171, which is still worse than 0.158. The per-place power-law road term is the other suspect.

**What this means for the plan (D15)**
- On PVS, the nuisance structure is captured better by a *scalar* model (one gain per vehicle plus a within-place speed elasticity) than by my spectral factorisation v0.
- This agrees with P1's small interaction term (about 2%).
- **The SCR spectral factorisation is demoted from "the instrument" to "an optional instrument that has not yet earned its place".**
- The identifiability *question* survives intact. The decisive facts in P1 (the 18% vehicle share and the sign-flipped speed elasticity) need no spectral model at all.

| Reason | Count |
|---|---|

**Cause 1: the training data teaches unidentifiable claims.**
- 2,284 of 2,696 training targets state event positions ("around the 1.2 km mark").
- Of 7,793 position mentions, only 602 (7.7%) appear anywhere in the input capsule; the capsule gives event counts and confidences, not positions.
- The model was trained to state values its input does not contain, the same identifiability failure as the road claims, one level up. This is the known "divergent reference" problem of data-to-text.

**Cause 2: synthetic-to-real shift and an input bug.**
- Synthetic capsules always have street names and map tags.
- The PVS capsules have neither, but still list `ch=acc,gyro,gps,map` (map coverage is 0). The model fills the gaps with invented names and tags.

**What it means for the paper**
- The verifier and fallback guarantee holds: no unsupported claim reaches the output.
- But the fine-tuned model contributes nothing on real trips.
- The honest headline is the diagnosis: a narrator trained on targets that state non-identifiable values learns to guess them.
