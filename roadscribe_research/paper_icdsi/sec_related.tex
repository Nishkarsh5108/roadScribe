\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Faithfulness and its checks.}
Faithfulness to the source and factuality with respect to the world are distinct properties of generated text~\cite{maynez2020faithfulness,ji2023hallucination}.
Data-to-text work measures faithfulness by extracting records from the text and checking them against the input table~\cite{wiseman2017challenges}. It separates content planning from realization to control it~\cite{moryossef2019step,gatt2018survey}, and checks captions of time series with executable programs~\cite{jhamtani2021truce}. Factuality can be scored per atomic claim~\cite{min2023factscore}.
RoadScribe's verifier and template fallback belong to this line.
In sensor-to-text generation, the gap between the two properties takes a specific form: the source is itself a measurement, and its value can depend on the device and on how it was used.
Our world-level test asks whether the source value is a property of the world, a question that checks of text against source cannot answer.

\paragraph{Unsupported targets and hallucination.}
References that state facts absent from the input (\emph{divergent references}) are a known source of hallucination in data-to-text generation.
Proposed remedies include:
\begin{itemize}
\item metrics that account for them~\cite{dhingra2019parent};
\item cleaning or re-annotating the data so that inputs and texts match~\cite{dusek2019semantic,nie2019simple};
\item training that controls how far outputs may go beyond the input~\cite{filippova2020controlled}.
\end{itemize}
Fine-tuning a language model on facts it cannot know from pre-training likewise increases hallucination~\cite{gekhman2024newknowledge}.
Our input-level test is a typed version of this diagnosis, done before training.
Our controlled experiment holds the targets fixed and changes only whether the input contains the values they state, so that the effect on each value type can be read directly.

\paragraph{Language models over sensor data.}
Language models have been prompted with raw sensor readings~\cite{xu2024penetrative}, aligned with motion-sensor encoders~\cite{li2025sensorllm,imran2024llasa}, given plots~\cite{yoon2024bymyeyes} and trained on synthetic time-series question answering~\cite{xie2025chatts}.
They have also been pre-trained on large wearable corpora with captions generated from signal statistics~\cite{zhang2025sensorlm}.
Their evaluations measure accuracy, retrieval or judged plausibility.
Such captions inherit whatever device dependence their statistics carry; our results quantify that dependence for one sensing task.

\paragraph{Vehicle response, calibration and invariance.}
Road sensing from vehicles goes back to Pothole Patrol and Nericell~\cite{eriksson2008pothole,mohan2008nericell}, and PVS has been used to classify surfaces and quality~\cite{menegazzo2021pvs}.
That the response depends on the vehicle and speed is established~\cite{dodds1973roughness,gonzalez2008roughness}, and crowdsourced methods invert vehicle models to estimate road spectra~\cite{botshekan2020roughness}.
Response-type roughness meters have long been calibrated per vehicle against reference sections~\cite{sayers1986iri}.
We do not propose a new roughness estimator or a new calibration. We use paired data to decide which claims a narrator may state, with a two-way fixed-effects model~\cite{abowd1999akm} and an invariance criterion across environments~\cite{peters2016icp}.
