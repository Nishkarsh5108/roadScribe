\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Road sensing from vehicles.}
Phones and in-vehicle units have been used to detect potholes and braking since Pothole Patrol and Nericell~\cite{eriksson2008pothole,mohan2008nericell}, and to classify surfaces or regress roughness from vibration, for example on the PVS dataset used here~\cite{menegazzo2021pvs}.
That the measured response depends on the vehicle and on speed is well established: road spectra and vehicle response are linked through the vehicle's transfer function~\cite{dodds1973roughness,gonzalez2008roughness}, and crowdsourced methods estimate road roughness spectra from phone acceleration by inverting a vehicle model~\cite{botshekan2020roughness}.
We do not propose a new roughness estimator.
We ask which of these quantities a \emph{narrator} may state as a fact about the road.

\paragraph{Language models over sensor data.}
LLMs have been prompted with raw sensor readings~\cite{xu2024penetrative}, aligned with motion-sensor encoders~\cite{li2025sensorllm,imran2024llasa}, given plots~\cite{yoon2024bymyeyes}, trained on synthetic time-series question answering~\cite{xie2025chatts}, and pre-trained on tens of millions of hours of wearable data paired with captions generated from statistics~\cite{zhang2025sensorlm}.
Their evaluation measures accuracy, retrieval or judged plausibility; the claims in their text are not checked against the measurements, and none of them asks whether a stated quantity can be determined from the input at all.

\paragraph{Faithful data-to-text.}
Separating content planning from realization is established in data-to-text generation~\cite{gatt2018survey,moryossef2019step}, and so is measuring precision by extracting records from generated text and checking them against the source table~\cite{wiseman2017challenges}.
Truth-conditional captioning checks time-series captions with executable programs~\cite{jhamtani2021truce}, and factuality can be scored per atomic claim~\cite{min2023factscore}.
Sensor-to-text systems with audience-specific output go back to BabyTalk~\cite{portet2009babytalk}, and driver-feedback systems turn telematics into text with rules and, more recently, LLM-written tips~\cite{braun2018saferdrive,baig2022drivingbeacon,thompson2026localising}.
RoadScribe's verifier and template fallback follow this tradition.
Our contribution is the observation that verification against a ledger cannot catch claims whose ledger value is not identifiable, and a measurement of how often this happens for road claims.
