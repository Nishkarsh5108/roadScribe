\section{Claim Identifiability}
\label{sec:ident}

\paragraph{Setting.}
A sensor-to-text system observes a recording $x$ of a world state $s$ (the road) under nuisance conditions $e$ (vehicle, speed, mount), computes ledger values $\phi(x)$, and states claims about them, for example the class $k(\phi(x))$ of a stretch.
A verifier that checks claims against $\phi(x)$ establishes \emph{faithfulness}~\cite{maynez2020faithfulness}, not that the claim is true of $s$.
We distinguish two ways in which a faithful claim can still be unfounded.

\paragraph{World level.}
A claim type is \emph{identifiable from the recording} if $k(\phi(x(s,e)))=k(\phi(x(s,e')))$, up to measurement noise, for the conditions $e,e'$ met in deployment.
\emph{Test:} on paired recordings of the same states under different conditions (several vehicles on the same places), compare the cross-condition agreement $\kappa_{\text{cross}}$ of the claimed classes with a \emph{noise floor} $\kappa_{\text{floor}}$, the agreement of two sensors under the \emph{same} condition, and compare the share of variance of $\log\phi$ explained by the condition (net of place and speed) with a placebo that shuffles condition labels within places.
A claim type is admissible when $\kappa_{\text{cross}}$ is close to $\kappa_{\text{floor}}$, with the tolerance fixed in advance.
This is an invariance test across environments in the sense of~\cite{peters2016icp}, applied to what a narrator may say.

Why vibration claims can fail it is standard.
For a linear vehicle with transfer function $H_c$ at speed $v$ over a place with elevation spectrum $\Phi_p(n)$ ($n$ in cycles/m), the vertical acceleration spectrum satisfies~\cite{dodds1973roughness,gonzalez2008roughness}
\begin{equation}
\log S(f)=A_c(f)+B_p(f/v)-\log v,\qquad A_c=\log|H_c|^2,\ B_p=\log\Phi_p .
\label{eq:scr}
\end{equation}
One vehicle at one speed fixes only $A_c+B_p$, so ``this road is rough'' and ``this vehicle transmits a lot'' are indistinguishable; vehicles that share places identify the differences $A_c-A_{c'}$, and the common part only if the same places are also driven at different speeds.
(In a simulation with three channels and thirty segments, a penalized Whittle fit~\cite{pawitan1994whittle} recovered the channel differences to within 0.025 log units, but the common channel only with speed diversity: error 0.010--0.023 against 0.67--1.09.)
For a band-integrated value this gives a two-way fixed-effects model, as used to separate worker and firm effects in matched data~\cite{abowd1999akm},
\begin{equation}
\log a_{wz,cp}=\alpha_p+g_c+\gamma\log\bar v_{cp}+\varepsilon_{cp},
\label{eq:fe}
\end{equation}
with place effect $\alpha_p$, vehicle gain $g_c$ and speed elasticity $\gamma$.
The gains are identified by shared places and $\gamma$ by speed variation \emph{within} places; estimated across places, $\gamma$ also absorbs how drivers choose speed on different roads.
The remedy is calibration on shared roads, long practised for response-type roughness meters~\cite{sayers1986iri}:
\begin{equation}
\tilde a_{\mathrm{cal}}=a_{wz}\,(v_{\mathrm{ref}}/\bar v)^{\hat\gamma}\,e^{-\hat g_c},\qquad v_{\mathrm{ref}}=10~\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}.
\label{eq:cal}
\end{equation}
For a vehicle that has never shared a road with others, the honest output is a statement about the ride, not the road.

\paragraph{Input level.}
A fine-tuned narrator learns a map from inputs $z$ to targets $y$. A value stated in $y$ is \emph{identifiable from the input} if it is a function of $z$; if the targets state values that $z$ lacks, training rewards producing values without evidence~\cite{dhingra2019parent,gekhman2024newknowledge}.
\emph{Test:} a typed audit of the training set that measures, for each value type the targets state (event positions, speeds, accelerations, distances, durations), the share of stated values that $z$ contains as a field of the same type, after rounding to the stated precision.
The prediction is per type: where the share is low, the model's claims of that type will be wrong at test time, whatever the verifier then does.
Both tests are cheap and model-independent; the first needs paired recordings, the second only the training set.
