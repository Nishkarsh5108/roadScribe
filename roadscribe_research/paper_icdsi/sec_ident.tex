\section{When Is a Road Claim Identifiable?}
\label{sec:ident}

A verifier can only confirm that a sentence agrees with the ledger.
Whether the ledger value is a property of the \emph{road} depends on what the data can separate.
For a linear vehicle with vertical transfer function $H_c$ driving at speed $v$ over a place $p$ whose elevation spectrum is $\Phi_p(n)$ ($n$ in cycles per metre), the vertical acceleration spectrum is~\cite{dodds1973roughness,gonzalez2008roughness}
\begin{equation}
S(f)=|H_c(f)|^2\,\Phi_p(f/v)/v,
\qquad
\log S(f)=A_c(f)+B_p(f/v)-\log v ,
\label{eq:scr}
\end{equation}
with $A_c=\log|H_c|^2$ and $B_p=\log\Phi_p$.
Three consequences follow.

\emph{One vehicle, one pass.}
At a single speed, any function can be moved between $A_c$ and $B_p$: the data fix only their sum.
``This road is rough'' and ``this vehicle transmits a lot'' are then indistinguishable.
This is the indeterminacy known from separating spectra shifted by different velocities in astronomy~\cite{simon1994disentangling,bedell2019wobble}; we use it as a criterion for claims, not as a new result.

\emph{Several vehicles on the same places.}
If vehicles share places, the place term cancels in their differences, so the \emph{relative} channels $A_c-A_{c'}$ are identified; the common part is identified (up to a constant) only if the same places are also driven at different speeds.
On synthetic data with three channels and thirty road segments, a penalized Whittle fit~\cite{pawitan1994whittle} of Eq.~\eqref{eq:scr} recovered the channel differences to within 0.02 log units in both cases, but the common channel only with speed diversity (error 0.010--0.023 versus 0.67--1.09 log units over three seeds).

\emph{Speed.}
For the band-integrated road value of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dec},
\begin{equation}
\log a_{wz,cp}=\alpha_p+g_c+\gamma\log\bar v_{cp}+\varepsilon_{cp},
\label{eq:fe}
\end{equation}
with a place effect $\alpha_p$ and a vehicle gain $g_c$.
The elasticity $\gamma$ is identified by speed variation \emph{within} places.
Estimated across places within one trip, it also absorbs how drivers choose speed on different roads.

\paragraph{Criterion and remedy.}
We call a road claim \emph{identifiable} from the narrator's input if its value is fixed by the data when the nuisances that can change (vehicle, speed) change.
A narrator that states a non-identifiable value as fact is guessing even if every sentence passes the verifier, because the ledger stores the same confounded value.
Equations~\eqref{eq:scr}--\eqref{eq:fe} also give the remedy for a fleet: estimate $\hat g_c$ and $\hat\gamma$ on places that several vehicles have driven, with place and vehicle fixed effects, and report
\begin{equation}
\tilde a_{\mathrm{cal}}=a_{wz}\,(v_{\mathrm{ref}}/\bar v)^{\hat\gamma}\,e^{-\hat g_c}.
\label{eq:cal}
\end{equation}
This makes road claims comparable across calibrated vehicles.
For a vehicle that has never shared a road with others, the road value remains unidentified, and the honest output is a statement about how the ride felt.
